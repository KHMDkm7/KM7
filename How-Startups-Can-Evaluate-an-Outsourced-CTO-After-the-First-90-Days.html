How Startups Can Evaluate an Outsourced CTO After the First 90 Days

The first 90 days of an outsourced CTO engagement can reveal whether the technical leadership relationship is working as expected. By this point, the CTO should have developed a meaningful understanding of the product, identified important technical concerns, and begun addressing the highest-priority issues.

However, evaluating the engagement should involve more than asking whether a certain number of tasks were completed. Founders should assess whether the CTO has improved technical visibility, decision-making, risk management, and alignment between engineering and business priorities.

Review the Original Objectives

Start by returning to the goals established when the engagement began.

Review whether the CTO has made meaningful progress on areas such as:

  • Technical assessment
  • Architecture improvements
  • Engineering processes
  • Development team management
  • Technical risk reduction
  • Product roadmap alignment
  • Infrastructure planning

The purpose is not to expect every original goal to be finished within 90 days. Some technical initiatives naturally require longer timelines. The important question is whether the CTO has created measurable progress toward them.

Evaluate Technical Visibility

One of the most important outcomes of technical leadership is better visibility into the state of the product.

After 90 days, founders should have a clearer understanding of:

  • How the product is architected
  • Where the major technical risks exist
  • What technical debt needs attention
  • Which infrastructure concerns matter
  • What the engineering team is working on
  • Which technical decisions are coming next

If the founder still cannot explain the major technical priorities, the engagement may need to be reassessed.

Review the Technical Roadmap

The CTO should have helped connect technical work with the company's product direction.

Review whether there is now a practical technical roadmap covering major areas such as architecture, infrastructure, security, technical debt, and upcoming product requirements.

The roadmap should also reflect realistic engineering capacity. A long list of technical initiatives without clear priorities does not necessarily indicate effective technical leadership.

Assess the Quality of Technical Decisions

Look at the important technical decisions made during the first 90 days.

Consider whether the CTO:

  • Explained the reasoning behind major decisions
  • Considered realistic alternatives
  • Identified important trade-offs
  • Considered long-term consequences
  • Connected technical decisions to business priorities

Strong technical leadership should make decisions easier to understand, even when the underlying technology is complicated.

Evaluate the Development Team Relationship

The CTO's relationship with developers is another important indicator.

Review whether developers now have:

  • Clear technical direction
  • Defined responsibilities
  • Appropriate autonomy
  • A process for escalating technical issues
  • Useful feedback on technical decisions

The CTO should provide direction without unnecessarily controlling routine implementation decisions.

Review External Development Management

If the startup uses external developers, agencies, or contractors, evaluate whether technical oversight has improved.

Look at whether the CTO has helped establish:

  • Clear development expectations
  • Code quality standards
  • Technical review processes
  • Better visibility into progress
  • Clear escalation procedures
  • Appropriate access and ownership

The goal is to ensure that external development remains aligned with the startup's product and technical requirements.

Assess Technical Risk Reduction

The CTO should have identified important technical risks during the initial assessment.

After 90 days, review whether those risks have been:

  • Resolved
  • Reduced
  • Documented
  • Assigned an owner
  • Included in future planning

Not every risk needs to disappear. Some risks may be acceptable if the startup understands them and has a reasonable mitigation strategy.

Review Documentation and Knowledge Sharing

A strong CTO engagement should leave the startup with better technical knowledge than it had at the beginning.

Review whether important information is now documented, including:

  • Architecture
  • Technical decisions
  • Infrastructure
  • Development processes
  • Technical risks
  • Vendor dependencies

This reduces dependence on individual developers and makes future technical transitions easier.

Evaluate Communication

Technical leadership also depends on how effectively complex information is communicated.

Founders should consider whether the CTO communicates:

  • Technical risks clearly
  • Important decisions in understandable terms
  • Changes to priorities promptly
  • Problems before they become emergencies
  • Technical progress against agreed objectives

The CTO does not need to eliminate technical complexity. They should make that complexity easier for the founder to understand and act upon.

Compare Planned Work With Actual Progress

Review the work that was expected during the first 90 days against what actually happened.

For delayed initiatives, determine why they were delayed.

Possible reasons include:

  • Changing product priorities
  • Unexpected technical problems
  • Insufficient development capacity
  • Unclear requirements
  • New information discovered during the engagement

A delay is not necessarily a failure. What matters is whether the CTO identified the cause and adjusted the plan appropriately.

Look for Better Technical Decision-Making Across the Company

The most valuable outcome may not be a specific technical project.

Look for evidence that the company is making better technical decisions overall. Developers may have clearer standards, founders may have better visibility, and product decisions may include technical considerations earlier in the process.

This indicates that the CTO is improving the company's technical operating model rather than simply completing isolated tasks.

Identify What Should Change

The 90-day review should also identify areas where the engagement itself needs adjustment.

For example, the startup may need:

  • More frequent CTO involvement
  • Less operational involvement
  • Greater focus on architecture
  • More support for engineering management
  • Different communication processes
  • Additional technical expertise

The engagement should evolve as the startup's needs change.

Decide on the Next 90 Days

The review should end with a clear set of priorities for the next stage.

These priorities might include:

  • Major architecture improvements
  • Product development support
  • Engineering team growth
  • Infrastructure improvements
  • Security initiatives
  • Technical debt reduction

Keeping the next phase focused prevents the engagement from becoming a collection of loosely connected technical activities.

Final Thoughts

The first 90 days provide a useful point for evaluating whether an outsourced CTO engagement is producing meaningful technical progress.

Founders should look beyond completed tasks and assess technical visibility, decision quality, risk management, engineering alignment, documentation, communication, and progress against business priorities.

A successful engagement should leave the startup with a clearer understanding of its technology and a more structured approach to making future technical decisions. The 90-day review then becomes a starting point for refining the relationship rather than simply deciding whether the engagement should continue.
