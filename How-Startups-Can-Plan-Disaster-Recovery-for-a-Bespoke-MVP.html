How Startups Can Plan Disaster Recovery for a Bespoke MVP

Disaster recovery is often associated with large enterprises, but startups building a bespoke MVP also need to consider what happens when something goes wrong. A database failure, infrastructure outage, accidental deletion, or deployment problem can interrupt access to the product and its data.

The goal is not to build an expensive disaster recovery system before the product has been validated. Instead, startups should identify the most important risks and establish practical measures for restoring the MVP when a serious failure occurs.

Identify What Must Be Recovered

Start by identifying the parts of the MVP that would be difficult or impossible to recreate after a major failure.

This may include:

  • Customer data
  • Transaction records
  • Uploaded files
  • Database records
  • Application configuration
  • Important infrastructure settings

Not every piece of the system has the same recovery importance. Focus first on information and components that are essential to the product's operation.

Identify the Most Likely Failure Scenarios

Disaster recovery planning becomes more useful when it is based on realistic risks rather than every theoretical failure.

Consider scenarios such as:

  • Database corruption
  • Cloud infrastructure outages
  • Accidental data deletion
  • Failed deployments
  • Compromised credentials
  • Loss of access to an important service

The relevant scenarios will depend on how the MVP is built and which external services it uses.

Define the Recovery Priority

Decide which parts of the application need to be restored first.

For example, the database and core application may be more important than an administrative reporting feature. Establishing priorities helps the development team focus recovery efforts on the workflows that matter most.
